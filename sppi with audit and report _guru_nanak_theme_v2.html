The Three Institutions of the Guru

Each SPPI dimension is grounded in a 15th-century reform of Guru Nanak Dev Ji

SANGAT · The Equal Assembly

Guru Nanak taught that divine truth is revealed in congregation, not hierarchy. In the Sangat, the poorest laborer and the highest official sat side by side — caste, creed, and gender dissolved in collective deliberation. SPPI's governance dimension measures whether institutional voice carries that same parity.

c. 1500 • Kartarpur Sahib ੧

PANGAT · The Equal Row

The Langar's Pangat — all humanity seated in one row to eat the same meal — dismantled Sutak: the ritual pollution doctrines that barred women and lower castes from shared space. Mata Sulakhani and the sangat itself became stewards of this radical equality. SPPI's distribution dimension measures who may freely touch the institution's resources.

c. 1481 • First Langar ੨

KIRAT & VAND · Honest Shared Labor

"Kirat Karo" — earn by honest labor; "Vand Chhako" — share what you earn. Guru Nanak rejected ascetic withdrawal and predatory accumulation alike, grounding dignity in productive work and mutual capital. SPPI's economic dimension measures whether women hold assets and whether care labor — the household's unseen Kirat — is honored.
